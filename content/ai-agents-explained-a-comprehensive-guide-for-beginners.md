> **TL;DR:** AI agents are software that pursues a goal on its own rather than following a fixed list of rules. Unlike a language model, which only predicts from static training data, an agent can plan, use tools like the web and APIs, draw on memory and outside knowledge, and execute actions on my behalf. That autonomy is powerful, but it also means humans need to keep some control over the process.

I'm Alfie Marsh, co-founder and CEO of Tool Flow AI. AI agents are becoming a more integral part of our lives, and the technology is evolving from simply responding to our commands to understanding and acting autonomously.

Here I unpack what AI agents are, how they work and why they might be a game changer in your life and career.

## What is an AI agent?

*[Watch from 0:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hLJTcVHW8_I&t=23s)*

An AI agent is a piece of software designed to perform tasks autonomously. Unlike traditional software that follows strict rules, AI agents make decisions based on their understanding of, and interactions with, the world. They use technologies like large language models, such as GPT from OpenAI, Claude from Anthropic or Gemini from Google, to process and understand information and determine the best course of action.

Imagine a digital assistant. You could tell it to ask a particular person whether they're available on a certain date and then send a calendar invite. It would do that, but you've given it a preset list of instructions. Instead, you could give it a more ambiguous goal: "I need to book some time with Joanne whenever I'm free in the next month or so, can you organise the schedules?"

The agent understands that objective and works out the other things it needs to do: step one, check your calendar for availability; step two, check Joanne's calendar; step three, determine the amount of time; and so on. They act more autonomously and can understand an objective, rather than follow a very specific set of rules.

## How agents differ from language models

*[Watch from 1:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hLJTcVHW8_I&t=96s)*

Agents are different to LLMs. Agents use models like GPT for understanding and generating language, but they can do much more.

Traditional language models predict responses based on the data they were trained on, and that data is static: they were trained on the internet and a bunch of other resources at a specific moment in time. They don't interact with the world beyond their training data. ChatGPT, for example, knows information only up until its last update, which as of today was December 2023, more than four months ago.

The model itself can't fetch or understand new events or data. If you asked who won the Max Holloway and Justin Gaethje fight at UFC 300 last weekend, and in what round, a language model isn't going to know. It will probably try to make something up to please you, and that's what leads to hallucinations.

Some language models have been integrating web search into their applications. ChatGPT-4 can access the internet with Bing, thanks to its partnership with Microsoft. But that isn't actually part of the language model; it's something they've programmed on top of it, which is a step towards agentic behaviour.

## How AI agents work

*[Watch from 2:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hLJTcVHW8_I&t=166s)*

AI agents are essentially sophisticated problem-solving machines that can plan, execute and learn from their actions. They are made up of several components:

1. **The ability to plan.**
2. **The ability to interact with tools.**
3. **The ability to have memory and store knowledge.**
4. **The ability to execute actions.**

Let's take a look at each one.

## Planning

*[Watch from 3:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hLJTcVHW8_I&t=188s)*

Everything starts with a goal, whether it's researching a market trend or drafting an email. An agent begins by defining what needs to be achieved. It then creates a detailed plan, breaking the goal down into manageable tasks, much like the chain-of-thought approach in prompt engineering. That means the agent knows not only what to do but also how to approach each task for optimal results.

Ultimately, it takes the human training and predefined triggers out of the process and comes up with them on its own.

## Interacting with tools

*[Watch from 3:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hLJTcVHW8_I&t=211s)*

Unlike basic language models, AI agents can interact with a variety of tools, which is part of how they interact with the external world around them. They can browse the internet, access databases and use APIs to gather information or perform tasks. This lets them extend their capabilities far beyond being a static data set.

## Memory and external knowledge

*[Watch from 4:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hLJTcVHW8_I&t=242s)*

Agents can also be equipped with specific, specialist knowledge, for example your company's data or market research that isn't publicly available. They use techniques like retrieval augmented generation, or RAG, which integrates external resources so the agent can go and capture information and bring it into the language model's responses. Effectively, it enhances the responses with more up-to-date or relevant information.

Say you go to a startup's website and type in a question. Instead of the language model trying to answer your very specific customer question with what it was trained on in a generic model, it uses retrieval augmented generation to search the database of possible questions and answers from that company's help desk, and integrates that answer into its response. The result is more up to date and more accurate.

## Executing actions

*[Watch from 5:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hLJTcVHW8_I&t=307s)*

Lastly, AI agents can execute actions. They can write reports, make emails and even manage other software applications. We are also entering a world where agents can start communicating with other agents that have been trained to perform specific things.

This autonomous execution is what sets them apart from more passive technologies. It's where people are thinking, wow, what can we do when we can automate work to the extent that we just explain what we want to happen and the rest is taken care of?

## The risks

*[Watch from 5:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hLJTcVHW8_I&t=339s)*

The future of AI does pose some risks. AI agents represent significant advancements in how we interact with technology, but they can't act independently. The fact that they come up with their own plan of tasks and then act on them autonomously could pose a threat.

Imagine you asked an AI agent to solve world peace, and its reasoning was that world peace was best achieved by killing all humans. Then maybe it goes off and kills all of humanity. Not the ideal outcome we'd want. So there is an element of control and interaction that humans have to maintain over this process to get good quality results.

Some people think that as models go from GPT-4 to GPT-5 and so on, their reasoning capability will improve a lot and help the output of AI agents become higher quality.
